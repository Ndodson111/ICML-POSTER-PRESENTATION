The Empirical Optimal \(m_\sigma\)

Finite-data training loss $$\min_\theta\;\mathbb{E}_{\sigma,X,Z}\,\bigl\|m_\sigma^\theta(X+\sigma Z)-X\bigr\|^2$$ On a finite dataset, the natural benchmark is the empirical-optimal denoiser \(m_\sigma\).
Empirical optimum = weighted average $$m_\sigma(x)=\sum_{i=1}^N w_i(x,\sigma)\,x_i$$ $$w_i(x,\sigma)=\frac{\exp\!\left(-\|x-x_i\|^2/(2\sigma^2)\right)}{\sum_{j=1}^N \exp\!\left(-\|x-x_j\|^2/(2\sigma^2)\right)}$$ The curve tracks \(W_\sigma(x)=\max_i w_i(x,\sigma)\).

Noise level decreases → one weight becomes concentrated → nearest neighbor behavior

We use the expected max weight as an estimate for when nearest neighbor behavior arises in the empirical optimal solution.
